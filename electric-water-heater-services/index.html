Electric water heaters fail in a few very specific ways, and the symptom usually tells you where to look. No hot water anywhere suggests a tripped breaker, a tripped high-limit reset, or a failed upper element or upper thermostat, because the upper side controls when the lower side gets power. A few minutes of hot water followed by lukewarm points to the lower element or lower thermostat. Water that comes out scalding points to a thermostat stuck closed or an element shorted to the tank, which is also what trips the red reset button. Those are the calls we answer most in Downers Grove, IL, and nearly all of them are fixable.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Rusty water, leaks around an element, or water at the base
  • Tap water much hotter than the thermostat setting
  • Browned wiring or a burnt odor from behind the access cover
  • The water heater breaker tripping over and over
  • A hissing or sizzling noise near the lower element
  • A shower that turns lukewarm much sooner than it used to
  • No hot water at all from any faucet
  • A red reset button that keeps tripping

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Two things make electric water heater repair trickier than it looks: the power involved and the overlap between symptoms. Every element and thermostat connects to 240-volt terminals, and those terminals stay live until the breaker is off and the circuit is tested dead. On the diagnostic side, a heater that will not heat might have a failed element, a bad thermostat, a tripped reset, or a wiring problem, and a reset that keeps tripping is a warning, not an annoyance. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Replace a worn anode rod before corrosion moves on to the tank itself
  • Test the temperature and pressure relief valve once a year
  • Make sure water flows steadily from a hot tap before the heater's power goes back on
  • In hard water areas, a softener or low-watt-density elements can extend element life
  • Treat a tripped high-limit as a warning sign, not a nuisance
  • Drain a few gallons from the bottom of the tank periodically to control sediment
  • Set both thermostats near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
