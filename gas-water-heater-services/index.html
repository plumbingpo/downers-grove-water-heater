A gas water heater is a refreshingly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. When something goes wrong, it is usually at one of those points. In Downers Grove, IL, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Downers Grove Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Downers Grove call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

Most gas water heater repairs are finished in a single visit. When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Gas water heaters are among the riskiest appliances to work on without training, because the biggest hazards are invisible. A fitting that is almost tight can seep gas for days before anyone notices. A vent connector that has lost its slope, or a flue partly blocked by debris, can let carbon monoxide spill into the house every time the burner fires, with no smell to warn you. And many repairs, including gas valve and heater replacement, require a permit and inspection in most areas. Relighting a pilot by following the label is something most homeowners can do; almost everything past that deserves a technician.

The value of calling a pro is certainty. We test instead of guessing, from thermocouple millivolt readings and burner flame checks to gas pressure at the valve and a draft test at the hood. Before we leave, every connection is leak-tested, the vent is confirmed to carry exhaust outside, and CO is checked. And because some gas heaters are not worth another repair, we give you a straight answer on repair versus replacement first, then handle either one with correct venting, gas piping, and the code items your installation requires.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
