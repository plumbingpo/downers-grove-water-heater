How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Start with the age. A tank past eight to twelve years has done most of its work, and the serial number on the label tells us exactly how old yours is. Then look at the symptoms. A leak from the tank itself, rusty water from the hot side only, and loud sediment noise all point to a tank near the end. A thermocouple, an element, or a relief valve can be swapped on almost any heater; a rusted-out tank cannot, and plenty of households in Downers Grove, IL usually find that out the hard way, with a wet floor.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Replacement

A water heater replacement touches more systems than most people expect. On the gas side there is the supply line, the sediment trap, the shutoff, and a vent that has to draft properly so exhaust never backs up into the house. Electrical work means matching breaker and wire gauge to the element wattage. The tank needs a relief valve of the correct rating piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models add their own demands: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

What you get from a pro is a replacement that is done once. Our technicians match the new heater to your hot water demand, check that the gas supply, venting, and panel can handle it, and take care of the permit when your jurisdiction requires one. We route the relief valve discharge correctly, add the code items that apply, test for leaks and proper draft, and take the old tank with us. Rushed swaps tend to leave missing expansion tanks, bad vent pitch, and undersized gas lines behind. Ours are built to pass inspection and to last. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
