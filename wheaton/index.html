How Wheaton Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many premature tank failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement.
