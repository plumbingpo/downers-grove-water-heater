Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Higher utility bills that point to a heater losing efficiency
  • Water temperatures that worry you before a health inspection
  • Rusty water or damp spots around the equipment
  • Unsteady delivery temperatures that suggest a failing mixing valve
  • Lukewarm water at the exact time you need the most
  • A commercial heater that has grown noticeably louder
  • Distant fixtures that take minutes to run warm
  • An aging unit you would rather replace on your schedule than in an emergency

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Replace consumed anode rods before the tank itself starts to corrode
  • Set a flushing schedule based on usage; busy tanks collect sediment fast
  • Log water temperatures at key fixtures so drift is caught early
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected
  • Plan replacement before an aging unit fails during peak season
  • Have mixing valves checked so fixtures stay neither scalding nor lukewarm
  • Do not store supplies against the equipment or block air openings
