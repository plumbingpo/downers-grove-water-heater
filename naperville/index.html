Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Naperville

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year if their water is hard.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a flooded utility room.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Whatever type of water heater your Naperville home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric tanks burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units show error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
