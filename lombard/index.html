How Lombard Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
